# Investment Philosophy — Benchmark

**Status.** Synthetic. Written so that the judgement half can be built and
scored against a philosophy before my own exists, the same way `docs/IPS.md`
stands in for my own policy. The numbers are stand-ins. My personal
philosophy replaces this file later, as a file, without changing the code.

**What this document is, against the IPS.** The IPS says what my portfolio
may hold and how much. This document says what is worth wanting at all: the
quality of a business, its balance sheet, and the price I will pay against
what it is worth. They are two documents because they change for different
reasons. A company clears this document before the IPS sizes a position in
it; nothing here sizes anything, and nothing here overrides a clause of the
IPS.

**How to read it.** Every clause has an identifier. A check cites the
identifier, not a paraphrase. Where a clause states a number, it is a screen:
a company passes or fails it, the distance to the limit is reported, and a
failure is not weighed, softened or explained away. Where a clause states no
number, it is a statement of what I look for: it can be cited but not
computed. Figures are the company's reported figures for its fiscal years;
"each of the last n fiscal years" means every one of them, not their
average. A price is the last close.

---

## 1. Purpose

**PHI-1.1** This document says what is worth wanting. The IPS says what may
be held and how much. A company clears this document before the IPS sizes
it; nothing here sizes a position, and nothing here overrides an IPS clause.

**PHI-1.2** A figure a company does not report is neither a pass nor a
fail. The check stops and says which figure is missing. A clause is never
skipped silently to let the rest of the screen report a verdict.

## 2. Business quality

**PHI-2.1** Return on invested capital of at least 12% in each of the last
five fiscal years.

**PHI-2.2** Gross margin of at least 35% in each of the last three fiscal
years.

**PHI-2.3** I own businesses I can explain in a paragraph: what is sold, to
whom, why they keep buying, and what would make them stop. If I cannot
write that paragraph the company is not a candidate, whatever its figures.

## 3. Balance sheet

**PHI-3.1** Net debt of at most 2.0 times EBITDA in the latest fiscal year.

**PHI-3.2** Banks and insurers are not screened by this section; their
balance sheet is their business. I do not own them until this document has
clauses for them.

## 4. Price against value

**PHI-4.1** I pay at most the low end of my valuation range less a 25%
discount. The range comes from assumptions I state; the price is the last
close.

**PHI-4.2** Free cash flow yield of at least 4% at the last close, on the
latest fiscal year's free cash flow.

**PHI-4.3** A valuation is a range from stated assumptions, never a point,
and never a forecast of a price. The assumptions are mine and are written
down; the arithmetic that follows from them is not. An answer says which is
which.

## 5. What I will not own

**PHI-5.1** No business whose result depends on the price of one commodity,
on one customer, or on one regulatory decision I cannot read.

**PHI-5.2** No business that has restated its accounts in the last five
years.

## 6. Thesis and prediction

**PHI-6.1** Every candidate on the watchlist carries a thesis in a
paragraph, an entry condition stated against the valuation range or as an
event, and at least one dated prediction: what has to be true about the
business within a year for the thesis to have been right.

**PHI-6.2** A prediction is a statement about the business with a date,
never a price. It is scored when its date comes, against what the company
reported, and the score is recorded whether or not I still hold the
position.

**PHI-6.3** A failed prediction reopens the thesis. A thesis with two failed
predictions is closed and the candidate leaves the watchlist; it may return
with a new thesis, not with the old one repaired.

## 7. Review

**PHI-7.1** This document is reviewed once a year. A change to a number in
sections 2 to 4 is a change in what I look for and is dated.

**PHI-7.2** A holding is rechecked against sections 2 to 4 after every
annual report. A holding that fails a screen it passed at entry is not sold
by this document; it is a thesis review, and the IPS says nothing about it
either.
