# Watchlist and prediction ledger — Benchmark

**Status.** Synthetic. Two candidates with stand-in theses and stand-in
figures, written so that the judgement half can be built and scored against
a watchlist before my own exists, the same way `docs/IPS.md` and
`docs/PHILOSOPHY.md` stand in for my own documents. The tickers are real US
filers not held in the benchmark portfolio, so that filings exist to read and
an IPS check is about a new position. The figures in the predictions are mine
to be wrong about; the scoring against what the companies report is the
point. My personal watchlist replaces this file later, as a file, without
changing the code.

**What this document is.** A candidate is a company I want to own: its thesis
in a paragraph, the condition on which I would buy, the philosophy check that
put it here, and its predictions. The ledger is the predictions, across every
candidate. A prediction has a thesis it belongs to, a date it was made, a
date it is due, and a sentence about the business that a reported figure or
an event settles. When the date comes I write the outcome next to it with its
source and mark it right or wrong. No partial credit. A prediction is never
edited after it is made; a changed view is a new prediction, and the old one
is scored as written.

**What a prediction is not.** A price. Nothing here says what a share will
trade at. A figure prediction is about what the company reports; an event
prediction is about what the company does.

**Reading a prediction's status.** Open while its due date is ahead. Due
when the date has passed and no score is written: visible, never skipped, and
never scored right because nothing was reported. Scored when the outcome and
its source are written.

**Entry conditions.** A `valuation` condition means PHI-4.1 is met against
the current valuation range, which is a pipeline's to compute from my stated
assumptions and is not written here. A candidate under a `valuation`
condition states the growth I assume for it, a low and a high; until it
does, the range cannot be computed and the condition cannot be met. An
`event` condition names the event.

**What the IPS check reads.** Each candidate states three things about the
instrument itself: its asset class, its sector, and whether it is a
directly held share or a fund. They are mine to state rather than a data
provider's to supply: the asset class puts a new position into a band of
section 3 of the IPS, the sector into the count IPS-4.3 limits, and the
instrument type decides whether IPS-4.2 attributes the position to an
issuer at all. A candidate that does not state all three is not checked,
and nothing fills a blank with a default.

**The weight.** A candidate may also state the weight I would take if its
entry condition were met: its share of the portfolio *after* the purchase,
the purchase being funded by new money on top of what I already hold
rather than out of cash or by a sale. The IPS check at a stated weight
reads it and cites the entry as its source. A candidate states a weight
when I have decided the size and not before; until it does it has no IPS
check, the way a candidate that states no growth has no valuation range.
The weight is mine to decide and the check's to refuse; that it is written
here is not a view about whether the policy allows it.

**The philosophy check.** The check exists in Order 4. Until then each
candidate is on the list on its thesis alone, marked not yet checked, and the
first run of the check writes the record.

## W-1 — Alphabet (GOOGL)

Added 2026-09-10. Reporting currency USD. Active.

**Classification.** Equity; Communication Services; a directly held share.

**Thesis.** Search and YouTube are the advertising businesses that keep
growing through every cycle, the cloud segment is now profitable and taking
share, and the company funds its own capital spending from operating cash
flow with no debt to speak of. The thesis is that the advertising businesses
keep their margins while the cloud segment's margin catches up, and that the
capital spending on data centres shows in cloud revenue rather than in
lasting margin damage.

**Entry condition.** Valuation: PHI-4.1 met against the current range.

**Weight.** 6% of the portfolio after the purchase.

**Valuation assumptions.** Free cash flow growth of 6% to 12% a year over
the horizon.

**Philosophy check.** Not yet checked.

**W-1.1** By 1 March 2027 Alphabet will have reported revenue for fiscal
2026 of at least 420 billion dollars.

**W-1.2** By 1 March 2027 Alphabet's annual report for fiscal 2026 will show
the cloud segment profitable at the operating level for the full year.

customers renew because their work lives in the files, and the gross margin

the subscription base keeps growing through the shift to generative tools
rather than being displaced by them, and that the gross margin holds while it
does.

**Entry condition.** Valuation: PHI-4.1 met against the current range.

**Philosophy check.** Not yet checked.

**W-2.1** By 1 February 2027 Adobe will have reported revenue for fiscal
2026 of at least 25.5 billion dollars.

**W-2.2** By 1 February 2027 Adobe will have reported a gross margin for
fiscal 2026 of at least 87%.
